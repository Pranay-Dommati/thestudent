## The Pythagorean Theorem

The Pythagorean theorem is one of the most fundamental concepts in geometry. It states that in a right-angled triangle, the square of the length of the hypotenuse (the side opposite the right angle) is equal to the sum of squares of the other two sides.

![Right Triangle Diagram](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d2/Pythagorean.svg/260px-Pythagorean.svg.png "A right triangle showing sides a, b, and hypotenuse c")

### The Formula

The theorem can be expressed mathematically as:

$$a^2 + b^2 = c^2$$

Where:
- $a$ and $b$ are the lengths of the two shorter sides (legs)
- $c$ is the length of the hypotenuse

### Applications

The Pythagorean theorem has countless real-world applications:

1. **Architecture and Construction**
   - Ensuring buildings are square
   - Calculating roof slopes
   - Determining diagonal measurements

2. **Navigation**
   - Finding the shortest distance between two points
   - GPS calculations
   - Map reading

3. **Computer Graphics**
   - Distance calculations in 2D/3D space
   - Collision detection in games
   - Image processing algorithms

### Practice Problem

Consider a ladder leaning against a wall. If the ladder is 13 feet long and its base is 5 feet from the wall, how high up the wall does it reach?

![Ladder Problem](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/82/Pythagorean_theorem_ladder.svg/300px-Pythagorean_theorem_ladder.svg.png "Ladder leaning against wall forming right triangle")

**Solution:**

Using the Pythagorean theorem:
$$5^2 + h^2 = 13^2$$
$$25 + h^2 = 169$$
$$h^2 = 144$$
$$h = 12 \text{ feet}$$

The ladder reaches 12 feet up the wall!
